    Anchored audit ledger

    Make the audit log provable.

    The hash chain in the console proves order. Here the log's entries are combined into a Merkle tree and the root is sealed in a small proof-of-work block, the same idea a blockchain uses. Then you can prove one entry belongs to a sealed block without showing the rest.

    Simulated in your browser with SHA-256. A real deployment would publish block roots to an external ledger or timestamping service.

    Architecture

    Five layers, each with one job.

    Separate reasoning from actionThe model proposes; the application validates and executes. It never holds credentials.
    Least privilegeNarrow permissions per tool. Read-only tools are kept apart from tools that change state.
    Evidence before verdictEvery verdict rests on tool output an analyst can inspect.
    Autonomy matches impactActs alone only when confident and when a mistake is cheap to undo.
    Everything is loggedPrompts, tool calls, decisions and approvals are stored for review.

    FAQ

    Straight answers.

    Is this a finished product?

    No. It is a final-year design study with an interactive simulation. The scenarios are constructed and the numbers on this page are design values, not measurements from a live SOC.

    Does the AI act on its own?

    Only for low-risk actions and only at high confidence, depending on the autonomy stage. High-impact actions such as isolating a host or disabling an account wait for a person.

    Where does the data on this page come from?

    The alerts, regions and agent statuses are generated in your browser to illustrate the design. Nothing is fetched from a real network or SOC.

    Is the admin login secure?

    No. It is checked inside your browser and exists to demonstrate role-based access. A real system needs server-side authentication, MFA and proper secrets handling.

    What if the AI is wrong or manipulated?

    That is the reason for layered defences: data delimiters, an instruction rule, a tool allow-list, a policy engine outside the model, analyst sampling and an audit log. Try the defence lab above.

    Are the model prices in the estimator real?

    They are editable placeholders. Prices change, so enter the current ones from your provider.
